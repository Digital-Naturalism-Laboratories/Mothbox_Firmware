Calibration

Before a session the Mothbox can autofocus and meter the scene itself, then reuse the result for every photo.

On: autofocus and auto-exposure run when the period below has elapsed. Off: only the manual focus and exposure values on this page are used.

Focus

The lens is set in diopters (1 ÷ metres): bigger numbers focus closer. This page shows the distance in centimetres beside each value so you never have to do that sum.

Used when auto-calibrate is off, and as the starting point when it is on. Cameras differ slightly in their own units, so treat this as a per-box value.

A safeguard against the camera locking onto something right in front of the lens. If an autofocus lands outside this range it is rejected and the previous focus is kept. The stock range, 6 cm to 250 cm, fits every normal Mothbox; tighten it for a box whose distance to the sheet you know.

Manual is normal: the Mothbox runs its own autofocus during calibration and then holds the lens still.

Exposure

Photos use a fixed, very short exposure so a passing insect does not smear. Brightness comes from gain and the flash, not from a longer shutter.

The sensor has a floor at full resolution (about 870 µs on the OwlSight at the 456 MHz link, 1119 at 360). Anything lower is clamped to it, and the real value is written into each photo’s EXIF.

1 to 16. Higher is brighter and noisier. Auto-calibrate overrides this when it is on.

Nudges the auto-exposure used during calibration. 0 is neutral; positive is brighter. −8 to +8.

Off is normal: the Mothbox locks the colour gains for its white LEDs so every photo matches.

Image

Auto is right for every box: the firmware flips on a DIY (camera mounted upside-down) and not on a Pro. Only force it if a photo comes out the wrong way up.

0 is off. 3 or more takes that many photos at different exposures. 1 and 2 also mean off.

How far each bracketed photo shifts from the middle exposure. Ignored unless HDR is on.

Two things that can make Mothboxes not work

      
    
        
  • Don’t open the CSV on the card with Excel, Numbers, Vim, Sublime or Notepad++. They can leave swap files and backups on the tiny boot partition and rewrite the file with different encodings and line endings. Use this page instead.
    • 
        
  • Always eject the card properly and wait until the computer says it is safe before pulling it out. Yanking it mid-write corrupts the boot partition, and then the Pi just blinks at you.
